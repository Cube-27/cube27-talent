# Content Strategy — Enterprise Managed Teams

## Audience and job

The primary reader is an enterprise talent-acquisition, workforce, people, or functional leader who needs specialist capacity without adding operational complexity. They are evaluating credibility, scope, risk, and whether the partner can stay useful beyond sourcing.

The secondary reader is a candidate assessing whether the opportunity and evaluation process are worth their time.

## Reference findings

- Toptal leads with a concrete hiring promise, breadth, proof, and a single dominant action.
- Gun.io converts operational pain into a specific outcome and makes its evaluation standard visible.
- Flexiple Managed Teams explains the relationship in plain language, separates hiring from lifecycle operations, and repeatedly reinforces one accountable partner.
- Randstad Enterprise frames talent acquisition as part of a broader workforce lifecycle and business-agility outcome.
- AMS presents AI as an assistant to human expertise and distinguishes flexible support from a transactional agency.
- Superhuman demonstrates strong typographic hierarchy, full-width colour fields, and restrained product-level motion.

These patterns inform structure and clarity only. Competitor claims, language, metrics, and visual compositions are not copied.

## Core proposition

**Build the team your business needs.**

Supporting message: Managed hiring for specialists, complete teams, and senior leadership, with evidence-backed shortlists and ongoing workforce compliance support.

Primary CTA: **Build your team**  
Form submission CTA: **Send requirement**  
Candidate CTA: **Apply to join**

## Proof hierarchy

1. Recorded role-appropriate assessment.
2. Interview conducted by someone who does the work.
3. Curated shortlist with decision context.
4. Employee lifecycle management after selection.
5. Approved scale metrics: 200+ positions, 150+ brands, 15+ years.

## Voice

- Direct, calm, precise, and commercially aware.
- Short declarative sentences; no invented customer story.
- Lead with the buyer outcome. Mention a mechanism only when it proves the claim.
- Keep section introductions to one short sentence.
- Do not narrate internal operations, coordination, or workflow.
- Use enterprise language only when it improves clarity.
- Avoid superlatives, vague transformation claims, “revolutionary,” “seamless,” “world-class,” and generic AI promises.

## Forbidden content

Public copy may describe benefit-level coordination of contracts, payroll, applicable local requirements, culture onboarding, and client-defined performance reviews. Do not publish contractual mechanics, legal advice, equipment terms, removal or replacement language, fees, guarantees, unsupported delivery times, certification ownership, fake profiles, unapproved proof, company-name repetition, role-count claims, engineering-only positioning, or count-based hiring-process terminology.
